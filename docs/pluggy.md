# Pluggy: what the API gives us and how lastro reads it

The M1 spike note. It answers the open questions from `FEATURES.md` with what Pluggy documents (docs.pluggy.ai, read on 2026-10-27) and with what my own connection returned on 2026-10-27: one MeuPluggy item (Nubank, a checking account and a credit card, 561 transactions, 115 investments), read with `lastrod fixtures`. Its anonymized copy is in `modules/ingest/testdata/meupluggy`; rerun the tool to refresh it (see `modules/ingest/testdata/pluggy/README.md`).

## The path: Meu Pluggy

- Each bank connected in Meu Pluggy becomes one **item** in my Pluggy application, through the MeuPluggy connector. These are proxy items: they reflect every refresh of the Meu Pluggy connection (every 24 hours), cannot be updated through the API and are not auto-synced on their own. lastro only reads.
- `GET /v2/items` is not available for Meu Pluggy accounts, so the item ids come from `LASTRO_PLUGGY_ITEM_IDS`.
- The authorization I give my application when signing in to Meu Pluggy **lasts one year** (`consentExpiresAt` on the item). After it lapses, reads come back **empty** while the item may still say `UPDATED`; the next update fails with `USER_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`. Renewing it means connecting MeuPluggy again in the Dashboard, and the item id may change.
- Free plan: personal use, accounts under my own CPF, up to 5 connections.

## Reading

| What | Endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| API key | `POST /auth` | Lives 2 hours. The client reuses it for 100 minutes and renews it once on a 401. |
| Item status | `GET /items/{id}` | `status`, `executionStatus`, `lastUpdatedAt` (when Pluggy last refreshed it), `consentExpiresAt`, `error.code`. |
| Accounts | `GET /accounts?itemId=` | `BANK` (checking, savings) and `CREDIT` (credit card). |
| Transactions | `GET /v2/transactions?accountId=&dateFrom=` | Cursor pages of up to 500; `next` is a ready-made query string. The page-based `GET /transactions` is deprecated on 2026-12-31. |
| Investments | `GET /investments?itemId=&page=` | Page numbers. |
| Investment transactions | `GET /investments/{id}/transactions?page=` | Page numbers, no date filter. `type`: `BUY`, `SELL`, `TAX`, `TRANSFER`, `INTEREST` (dividends, coupons) or `AMORTIZATION`; `date` (made) and `tradeDate` (settled), `quantity`, `value` (unit price), `amount` (gross), `netAmount` and, from the brokerage note, `expenses`. Replaces the deprecated `transactions` inside an investment. |

Rate limits are per IP and per endpoint: 360 requests a minute for each read, which a daily sync of five items never approaches. A 429 carries `RateLimit-Reset`; the client waits it out up to three times.

The limits that matter are the Open Finance ones, on Pluggy's side of the connection (per CPF, per bank, per month: 4 calls for 12-month history, 240 for the last 7 days, 30 for card bills). They are spent by Meu Pluggy's daily refresh, not by lastro: lastro reads what Pluggy already stored, as often as it likes.

## Answers to the open questions

| Question | What the docs say | Confirmed with my data |
|---|---|---|
| How much history does the first sync return? | Up to 12 months (365 days) on the first collection; later refreshes merge the recent days (the last 7 for cards, up to 4 days before the last update otherwise). | More than that: Pluggy keeps what it collected since the connection began. Checking goes back to 21 Mar 2025 and the card to 3 Mar 2025, about 19 months. The card also has installments dated up to Aug 2027. |
| How do card bill payments arrive? | On the card, as a `CREDIT` with a negative amount and `operationType` `PAGAMENTO_FATURA`. When the bank does not report it, Pluggy builds it from the bill data as "Pagamento recebido", also `PAGAMENTO_FATURA`. On checking, the payment is an ordinary outflow (no operation type says "card bill"). | Mostly by description: the card has 35 "Pagamento recebido", only 6 of them with `PAGAMENTO_FATURA`, so the description rule is what catches them. But 12 of the 35 are Pluggy's own copies: a date-only payment (03:00 UTC) next to Nubank's with its time and the same amount, on the same day. Ingest leaves those out (decision 14). Of the 23 left, checking's 22 "Pagamento de fatura" outflows pair with the card side; the last one predates checking's history. |
| How do installments arrive? | One transaction per installment with `creditCardMetadata.installmentNumber` and `totalInstallments`. Banks differ: some post every installment at once (later ones dated on their bill), most post one each month. There is no id that groups the installments of one purchase, and `totalAmount` is not sent on Open Finance. | 59 installments, from 2 to 12 per purchase. Every future installment is already there, dated in its own month and pending, so each one counts in the month it falls in. |
| How do pending transactions behave? | `PENDING` is a card purchase on an open bill, a future installment, or a scheduled or blocked checking movement (scheduled ones are dated in the future). They usually become `POSTED` keeping their id. | 39 pending on the card (the open bill and future installments, 19 of them dated after today), none on checking. All 198 posted card transactions carry a `billId`. |
| Are ids stable? | No. Pluggy keeps the id when its match key (the bank's id, or description + amount + date) still matches. When description, amount or date change, typically around a card bill closing, the transaction is deleted and created again with a new id, and nothing links the two. `providerId` (the bank's own id, Open Finance only) is the stable anchor. A transaction can also vanish for a few days and come back with a new id. | No anchor at all: MeuPluggy is a direct connector (`isOpenFinance` false), so none of the 561 transactions has `providerId`. Re-keying by bank reference never applies here; a restated transaction shows up as one removed and one new. That matters from M2 on, when a category set on the old one would be left behind. |
| Which investments come through? | Whatever the bank shares over Open Finance: CDBs (including Nubank's Caixinhas and PicPay's Cofrinhos), LCI/LCA, Tesouro Direto, funds, stocks, BDRs, FIIs and ETFs, with `type` and `subtype`. On Open Finance `amountProfit` and the performance rates are null. PGBL and VGBL do not come through. | 115 from Nubank: 37 active CDBs (and 72 fully withdrawn), 3 stocks, 2 ETFs and 1 Tesouro bond. Stocks and ETFs held at Nubank do come through, so the B3 trade statement import is only needed for a broker that is not connected. |
| Do investment transactions come through? | They should: on Open Finance the expenses come from the regulated brokerage note (withheld income tax in `incomeTax`, every other tax in `other`). | Yes, with gaps (dump of 2026-10-27). 178 movements on 101 of the 115 investments, from 2025-03-21, the same start as the account history: 88 `BUY` (`movementType` `CREDIT`), 88 `SELL` (`DEBIT`) and 2 `TRANSFER`; no `INTEREST`, so dividends do not come this way, and no `expenses`. `date` and `tradeDate` are always the same day. **CDBs reconcile:** a CDB's first `BUY` falls on its `issueDate`, and buys less withdrawals equal what it holds, for every active CDB with a purchase (26). Movements arrive weeks late, and some never do: the 7 CDBs bought since September have none yet, 3 older ones have none (2 bought before the history starts, 1 in May), and 2 of the 72 fully withdrawn lack their `SELL`; for those, `issueDate` and `amountOriginal` remain. **Stocks do not reconcile:** the two held before October start with a `TRANSFER` (moved in from another broker: an amount, no quantity), and their `BUY`s explain under half of the shares held; the three bought in late October have no movements. Nubank also sends its ETFs as `EQUITY`/`STOCK`. |
| What do dates mean? | ISO 8601 in UTC; to read a time as Brazilian time, convert to GMT−3. | Almost every date has a time. 74 are exactly 03:00 UTC (midnight in São Paulo, so a plain day) and most of the rest are real times of day. Only 3 are midnight UTC. Converting to São Paulo before taking the day is right for all of them. |

## Decisions this leads to

1. **Ingest reads a window, not a delta.** Each sync reads every transaction of each account dated on or after a start date: the whole history on an item's first sync (or with `lastrod sync --full`), and the last 90 days afterwards. Reading the window again is cheap and catches pending → posted changes, restatements and late installments, which webhooks would otherwise be needed for.
2. **The window is the truth for its dates.** A transaction lastro has inside the window that Pluggy no longer returns is removed (soft-deleted), so vanished pending transactions and the old half of a delete-and-recreate disappear. An empty read never removes anything: that is what a lapsed authorization looks like.
3. **Transactions are keyed by Pluggy id, and re-keyed by the bank's id when there is one.** A new Pluggy id whose `providerId` matches a transaction lastro already has (live or removed) updates that transaction instead of creating one, so its category survives the delete-and-recreate. MeuPluggy sends no `providerId`, so there a restatement is a removal plus a new transaction; M2 has to decide whether to carry a manual category across by attributes.
4. **Direction comes from `type`, not from the sign.** `DEBIT` is money out and `CREDIT` money in, on cards too (Pluggy normalizes card purchases to `DEBIT`). In the ledger an amount is negative when money leaves the account, for checking and for cards alike.
5. **Amounts are read as decimals, never as floats.** The client keeps the JSON literal and ingest converts it to cents, rounding half away from zero. Purchases in another currency use `amountInAccountCurrency`, so every ledger amount is in the account's currency.
6. **A date at midnight UTC is a plain date**; any other time is converted to America/São Paulo before taking the day. My connection confirms it: plain days arrive as 03:00 UTC, midnight in São Paulo.
7. **Card bill payments**: the card side is recognized from `PAGAMENTO_FATURA` (or a "pagamento recebido/de fatura" description) and the checking side by the `TransferMatcher` pairing it with that card inflow. **Investment redemptions** (`RESGATE_APLIC_FINANCEIRA`) are transfers; yields (`RENDIMENTO_APLIC_FINANCEIRA`) are income. Nubank's "Valor recebido de Investimentos" is a redemption too (Pluggy files it under "Third party transfers"), but only 8 of my 30 carry the operation type, so the description decides. "Resgate de Cashback" is a reward, not my own money moving, so it stays income.
8. **Nothing that identifies a person is decoded.** The client reads ids, amounts, dates, descriptions and enums; owner, CPF and `paymentData` stay only in the raw payload stored in the `ingest` schema.
9. **Freshness comes from Pluggy.** "Last synced" on screen is `lastUpdatedAt` of the item (when Meu Pluggy last refreshed the bank), not when lastro last read it. An item older than 2 days, in `LOGIN_ERROR`, with an `error`, or past `consentExpiresAt` gets the stale-sync notice.
10. **A MeuPluggy item is named after its bank.** Its connector is `MeuPluggy` (id 200), not the bank, so ingest takes the bank from the COMPE code that starts its checking account's transfer number (260 is Nubank), then from an account name.
11. **A card keeps what the bank says about it.** `creditData` gives the limit, what is available and the bill's due day; Nubank sends no closing day (`balanceCloseDate` is null), so lastro shows none rather than guessing one.
12. **Installments are grouped by description, count and amount.** Pluggy has no id for a purchase. Nubank appends " 3/12" to each installment's description, and the first installment can differ from the others by a few cents of rounding. `creditCardMetadata.purchaseDate` would be the natural key, but on future installments it holds the installment's own date, so it is not used. Grouping by the description without the suffix (ignoring case: Nubank wrote one installment in capitals), the installment count and the amount (within a cent per installment) turns my 59 installments into 10 purchases.
13. **`descriptionRaw` adds nothing on MeuPluggy:** it equals `description` on all 561 transactions, so the ledger does not store it.
14. **Pluggy's copies of card bill payments are left out.** When the bank reports a bill payment, Pluggy can still build one from the bill data: a date-only "Pagamento recebido" with the same amount on the same day as the bank's, which has its time. Counted, every bill would be paid twice. The raw store keeps both; the ledger gets the bank's. A date-only payment without such a twin is the only record of it and stays.
15. **A reprocess is authoritative from the first stored transaction.** It rebuilds each account from every raw record Pluggy still returns, so what the records no longer hold (a copy left out by a newer rule) is removed, whatever its date. A normal sync removes only inside its 90-day window.
